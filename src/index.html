What To Learn First

This is the high-yield version: learn a few safe wines, learn why they work, then connect them to the plates guests actually order.

Five safe first recommendations

  • Blue Mountain Brut: oysters, fries, crudo, burrata, seafood. Bubbles plus acid makes salt and fat feel lighter.
  • Hiedler Loss Gruner Veltliner: green, tangy, spicy, yogurt, tahini, cucumber, herbs. Crisp and peppery without being weird.
  • Darting Durkheimer Fronhof Riesling: chili, jalapeno, honey spice, pickle, sausage. Do not oversell sweetness until tasted.
  • Bindi Sergardi La Boncia Sangiovese: tomato, pizza, pasta, salami, steak. Sour-cherry acidity is your tomato friend.
  • St. John Claret: steak, ribeye, mushrooms, duck, richer pasta. Black-fruit Bordeaux shape without needing a speech.

Server wine translator

  • High acid: fresh, bright, mouthwatering.
  • Tannin: dry grip, like strong black tea.
  • Mineral: salty, stony, clean.
  • Medium body: not heavy, not thin.
  • Fruit-forward: fruity does not automatically mean sweet.
Salt Salt makes wine taste softer and fruitier. Fries, oysters, olives, and salami love bright wine.
Fat Fat wants acid, bubbles, tannin, or bitterness. Cheese, steak, duck, and fried food need a reset.
Spice Heat can make alcohol and tannin feel hotter. Use Riesling, rose, beer, or fruit-forward cocktails.
Intensity Match weight. Light seafood wants fresh whites; ribeye wants Claret, Nebbiolo, Monastrell, or richer cocktails.
Ask Find the lane first: crisp, richer, red, cocktail, or no alcohol.
Match Use the dish weight, salt, fat, spice, and sauce before naming a drink.
Explain Give one reason only: acid cuts fat, bubbles refresh, tannin handles steak, or Riesling calms heat.
Confirm Mention allergies, sweetness, stock, or premium bottle details only after checking when needed.

Service Drill

Practice The Table Moment

Use this when you want active recall instead of reading. Pick a lane, answer out loud, then reveal the pairing, table phrase, and confirm note.

How to answer

  • Name the lane: crisp white, richer white, light red, steak red, cocktail, beer, or zero-proof.
  • Give one reason: acid, bubbles, tannin, salt, fat, spice, or intensity.
  • Say it like service: short, confident, and easy for the guest to understand.
  • Confirm: allergies, 86s, sweetness, egg whites, milk wash, raw seafood, and bottle details.

Wine Guide

By-The-Glass Confidence Builder

The goal is not to sound like a textbook. The goal is to know the style, know the best foods, and have one natural sentence ready.

Common guest ask translator

When a guest asks for a familiar varietal, anchor them first: "We do not have that exact glass, but I can put you in the same lane." Then choose by weight: crisp white, round white, light red, juicy red, steak red, bubbles, or rose.

Search helpers: Cab, Cabernet, Merlot, Malbec, Shiraz, Syrah, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Grigio, Pinot Gris, Chardonnay, Chablis, Prosecco, Champagne, Riesling, Moscato, Chianti, Rioja, Zinfandel, house white, house red.

Chardonnay Bodega Cerron Remordimiento Blanco by the glass. Eleonore Moreau Petit Chablis, Mount Eden Wolff Vineyard, or Meursault for a bottle. Same grape. Fuller white lane with pear, ripe lemon, and enough roundness for seafood, butter, trout, cod, crab, and creamy pasta. It may not read as a heavy butter-and-vanilla Chardonnay. Say: "This is our fuller Chardonnay-style glass; if you want a more classic premium Chardonnay bottle, I can show you Chablis, Mount Eden, or Meursault."
Oaky or buttery Chardonnay Bodega Cerron Blanco if they want a glass and food-friendly richness. Mount Eden Wolff Vineyard, Domaine de la Rochette Macon-Bussieres, or Meursault. Rounder body, ripe citrus, pear, and texture. Works with garlic bread, shrimp and crab, cod, trout, and ricotta dumplings. Be honest: "It is the richer white lane, but not a sweet butter bomb. If you want more premium Chardonnay depth, the bottles are stronger."
Sauvignon Blanc Hiedler Loss Gruner Veltliner for a crisp, bright glass. Denizot Sancerre if they want Sauvignon Blanc by the bottle; Landron Muscadet for seafood crispness. High freshness, citrus, green/herbal lift, and a clean finish. Great for cucumber, herbs, seafood, oysters, and tangy dishes. Gruner is less grassy and more white-peppery. Say: "If you like Sauvignon for crispness, I would go Gruner by the glass; if you want the exact grape, Sancerre is our bottle lane."
Pinot Grigio / Pinot Gris Wagner Stempel Weissburgunder for a soft, easy white. Hiedler Loss for a brighter crisp style; Maloof Where Ya Pjs At? for a skin-contact Pinot Gris bottle. Light-to-medium feel, pear/apple fruit, and an easy-drinking shape. Good with chicken, cheese, potatoes, salads, and lighter snacks. Pinot Blanc is softer and calmer than many sharp Pinot Grigios. Say: "This is the comfortable easy white; if you want more zip, I would move you to the Gruner."
Riesling Darting Durkheimer Fronhof Riesling by the glass. Donnhoff Feinherb by the bottle for a more classic German Riesling lane. Peach, lime, racy freshness, and a strong match for chili, pickle, honey spice, pork sausage, sprouts, and salty food. Do not promise bone-dry or sweet until tasted. Say: "Riesling is our best problem solver for spice; it has fruit and freshness, and I can confirm the sweetness level for you."
Moscato / sweet white Darting Riesling if they want fruit and freshness with a table wine. Valdespino Cream Sherry, Tawny Port, or dessert wine lanes after food. Riesling gives peachy fruit and aromatic lift, which can satisfy guests asking for a softer white. There is not a true Moscato-style glass. Say: "We do not have a sweet Moscato, but Riesling is the closest fruity white. For dessert, I can move you into sherry or port."
Rose Ameztoi Rubentis for bright, dry, coastal rose with a tiny spritz. Leitz Eins Zwei Dry Rose for cleaner red berry and less salty/spritzy character. Dry, refreshing, pink, seafood-friendly, and easy with salads, crudo, burrata, pizza, squash, and chicken. Ameztoi is more saline and energetic; Leitz is more classic and easy. Say: "Ameztoi is the fun coastal rose; Leitz is the clean dry rose."
Prosecco / Champagne Blue Mountain Brut for dry, crisp bubbles by the glass. Griesel Blanc de Noirs or Christophe Mignon Adn de Meunier Brut Nature for a premium bottle; Fattoria Moretto Lambrusco for sparkling red. Bubbles, acid, freshness, and celebration feel. Great with oysters, fries, crudo, burrata, seafood, and salty snacks. Blue Mountain is drier and more Champagne-shaped than Prosecco. Say: "If you want bubbles, Blue Mountain is our crisp BC brut; if you want Champagne energy in a bottle, I can show you the Blanc de Noirs or Meunier."
Pinot Noir Deinhard Deidesheim Pinot Noir by the glass. Pierre Thibert Chorey-les-Beaune for Burgundy; Kistler for premium Russian River Pinot. Light red body, cherry/strawberry fruit, soft tannin, and an earthy lane for mushrooms, carpaccio, duck, trout, and lighter red drinkers. The German Pinot is lighter and brighter than many California Pinots. Say: "This is the soft, lighter red. If you want a richer Pinot bottle, I would go Burgundy or Kistler."
Cabernet Sauvignon St. John Claret by the glass. White Rock Cabernet Sauvignon, Chateau Larose Perganson, or Aia Vecchia Sor Ugo by the bottle. Dark fruit, structure, cedar/savoury Bordeaux shape, and enough grip for strip steak, ribeye, mushrooms, duck, and richer pasta. Claret is Cabernet blended with Merlot, so it is a little softer than a big Napa Cab. Say: "For a Cab drinker, Claret is the safest glass; if you want true Napa Cabernet, White Rock is the bottle."
Merlot St. John Claret for the glass, because it includes Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon. Whitehall Lane Merlot for the exact grape by the bottle. Plum, dark fruit, smoother texture than straight Cabernet, and a steak-friendly red that is not too aggressive. Claret is more savoury/Bordeaux than plush California Merlot. Say: "This is the Merlot-Cab blend by the glass; if you want a true Merlot bottle, Whitehall Lane is the move."
Malbec Bodega Cerron Remordimiento Tinto for a darker, fuller red glass. St. John Claret for a more classic steak-red shape; Gulfi Nerojbleo for a bigger dark-fruit bottle. Plum, blackberry, spice, fuller body, and a good match for lamb, sausage, ribeye, squash, and smoky richness. It is Monastrell/Garnacha, not Malbec, so it is more Spanish, smoky, and savoury. Say: "If you like Malbec for dark fruit and body, this Spanish red is the closest glass."
Shiraz / Syrah Bodega Cerron Tinto if they want a dark, spicy red by the glass. Texier Brezeme Syrah for the exact grape by the bottle; Gigondas for a Southern Rhone blend with Grenache/Syrah/Mourvedre. Dark fruit, spice, savoury depth, and enough weight for lamb, sausage, ribeye, and smoky dishes. Australian Shiraz is often plusher; Texier Syrah is more savoury and peppery. Say: "By the glass I would use the Spanish red; for true Syrah, Texier is the bottle lane."
Chianti / Sangiovese Bindi Sergardi La Boncia by the glass. Castello di Potentino Sacromonte or Aia Vecchia Sor Ugo if they want a richer Italian bottle. Exact Sangiovese lane: sour cherry, herbs, tomato-friendly acidity, and a natural fit for Margherita, pasta, salami, and ricotta dumplings. It is fresher and more food-driven than jammy. Say: "This is the classic tomato-and-pizza red; if the table is going richer, I can move you into a bigger Italian bottle."
Zinfandel Sindicat la Figuera Grenache for juicy red fruit and warm spice. Bodega Cerron Tinto for darker fruit and more body. Generous fruit, spice, and comfort with salami, sausage, lamb, duck, and honey-chili pizza. These are less jammy and less high-alcohol than many Zinfandels. Say: "We do not have Zin, but this Grenache gives you the juicy, spicy red-fruit lane."
Rioja / Tempranillo Bindi Sergardi Sangiovese for red fruit, herbs, and food-friendly acidity by the glass. Algre Valgañon for Tempranillo & Grenache by the bottle. Red-fruit savoury profile, herbs, and a flexible pairing lane for tomato, salami, roasted meat, and mushrooms. No Tempranillo by the glass. Say: "For Rioja energy by the glass, I would use Sangiovese for freshness or Grenache for warmth; the exact Rioja-style bottle is Algre Valgañon."
Chablis Hiedler Loss Gruner Veltliner if they need a crisp, mineral-feeling glass. Eleonore Moreau Petit Chablis for the exact Chablis lane by bottle. Freshness, citrus, clean seafood compatibility, and a leaner feel than round Chardonnay. Gruner is not Chardonnay, but it solves the same crisp seafood-white request. Say: "If Chablis is the ask, the bottle is exact; for a glass, I would put you in the crisp Gruner lane."
Bordeaux / red blend St. John Claret by the glass. Chateau Larose Perganson for Bordeaux; Aia Vecchia Sor Ugo for a polished Super Tuscan blend. Merlot/Cabernet structure, dark fruit, savoury edges, and steak-table confidence. This is one of the cleanest matches. Say: "Claret is the Bordeaux-style blend by the glass; if you want to build the table around a bottle, Larose Perganson is the direct upgrade."
House white / easy white Wagner Stempel Weissburgunder for soft and easy; Hiedler Loss for crisp and bright. Dona Matilde Branco for a flexible food white with citrus, herbs, and texture. Approachable white wine without needing a long explanation. Ask one question: "Do you want softer and pear-like, or crisp and bright?" Then choose Wagner for soft or Gruner for crisp.
House red / easy red Bindi Sergardi Sangiovese for pizza/pasta; Deinhard Pinot Noir for lighter red. St. John Claret if the table is ordering steak, mushrooms, or richer mains. Easy red choices by food lane: tomato, light red, or steak red. Ask one question: "Are we pairing with pizza/pasta, or something richer like steak?" Then move them to Sangiovese, Pinot, or Claret.

Safety notes to confirm with the team

  • Spaghetti Western contains nuts.
  • Short Film carries a sesame allergy note.
  • Heartbreak Mountain and Lovers Mountain are milk washed.
  • Cloud 9 lists egg whites; confirm egg-white allergy and vegan language before recommending.
  • Oysters and Tuna Crudo are raw seafood; Wood Roasted Cod includes shellfish broth; Shrimp & Crab contains shellfish.
  • Food nut flags include Cashews, Burrata Cheese with pine nuts, Spiced Beet Salad with pistachio, Endive with pecan, Smashed Cucumbers with almond, and Winter Squash with peanut pesto.
